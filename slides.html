Station 2 · Strategy Sort 7 min

Four Ways to Price a Chocolate Bar

Value-based

Price = what customers believe it's worth. Brand, quality, status, experience.

Competitive

Price at, a bit above, or a bit below what rivals charge for similar products.

Cost-based

Per-unit cost + desired per-unit profit = price.

Penetration

Launch low to pull customers from established rivals and grab market share.

Station 3 · Cocoa Crunch 8 min

Who Has Pricing Power?

Pricing power: the ability to raise prices without losing too many customers.

Elastic demand: buyers cut back a lot when price rises → low pricing power.

Inelastic demand: buyers keep buying → high pricing power.

Station 3 · Market Conditions

What Shakes Pricing Power?

  • Supply-chain issues: 2024 cocoa shortages pushed prices to record highs.
  • Inflation: rising costs pressure businesses to raise prices.
  • Recession: customers cut back on treats and wants.
  • Competition & substitutes: more choices = more elastic demand.

Shrinkflation: same price, smaller bar. When costs rise but pricing power is weak, some companies shrink the product instead of raising the price.

Smart strategy or sneaky? What does it do to customer trust?

Station 5 · The Legal Line 5 min

Where the Law Draws the Line

Price fixing

Competitors may not collude to agree on a price.

Price gouging

No raising prices because demand jumped in a crisis (illegal in many states).

Price discrimination

No different prices based on race, nationality, sex or another protected status.

Predatory pricing

No pricing below cost to drive rivals out of business.

A high price is not automatically illegal. Raising Valentine's gift box prices because demand is high? Legal.
